# Manifesto

## The shift

Software has always moved toward higher abstraction — every
new generation of languages, tools, and platforms hides the
complexity beneath it and lets engineers think at a higher
level. Agents continue that arc. What was implicit craft
must become codified practice.

Engineering knowledge has always lived in people's heads —
learned by doing, lost when people move on. Codifying
practices and workflows for AI to follow is what turns
craft into efficiency, repeatability, and predictability.

## The problem

Most AI-assisted development is ad-hoc. Engineers prompt an
agent, get code back, and stitch it together manually. Good
patterns are discovered and lost. Bad patterns are repeated
across projects. There is no shared structure for how agents
should work, what they are responsible for, or why past
decisions were made.

## The response

Open Kata codifies practices so they can be taught, shared,
and reused. Agents get clear workflows. Engineers keep
control of the decisions. Context survives across sessions
and projects.

Three principles guide the design:

- **Humans govern, agents execute.** The framework
  coordinates; the engineer is always responsible for
  the outcome.
- **Portable by default.** Kata follow the Agent Skills
  specification and work with any agent that supports it.
  No coupling to specific tools, platforms, or MCP servers.
- **Explain the why.** Practices carry their reasoning so
  agents and future humans understand the intent, not just
  the rule.
